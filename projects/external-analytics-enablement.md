<section class="page-intro">
	<p class="intro-label"><a class="breadcrumb-link" href="{{ site.baseurl }}/projects/">Projects</a> / External Analytics Enablement</p>
	<h1>External Organization Analytics Enablement</h1>
	<p>This project turned a manually maintained workbook into defensible, leadership-ready reporting. It is a strong example of validation-first analytics work where the right answer was not more modeling complexity, but better trust boundaries, clearer reporting, and a realistic path forward.</p>
</section>

<section class="section-block">
	<div class="case-study-shell">
		<div class="detail-stack">
			<article class="detail-section">
				<p class="card-kicker">Business problem</p>
				<h2>Why direct analysis was risky</h2>
				<p>The organization had meaningful operating data, but it was maintained in a form optimized for day-to-day use rather than analysis. Workbook tabs contained mixed row types, text-labeled dates, inconsistent year labels, and source totals that could not be accepted at face value.</p>
				<p>The first challenge was not dashboard design. It was determining what the data could support with reasonable confidence and where reporting needed caution.</p>
			</article>

			<article class="detail-section">
				<p class="card-kicker">Architecture</p>
				<h2>How the work was structured</h2>
				<div class="detail-flow">
					<div class="detail-flow-step">
						<strong>Source review and validation planning</strong>
						<span>The workbook was inspected first to identify structural risks and define what had to be verified before any analysis could be trusted.</span>
					</div>
					<div class="detail-flow-step">
						<strong>Cleaned analytical datasets</strong>
						<span>Attendance, baptism, and event datasets were rebuilt with cross-reference checks back to the original workbook.</span>
					</div>
					<div class="detail-flow-step">
						<strong>Leadership-ready reporting and recommendations</strong>
						<span>Validated findings were shaped into executive-facing presentation material and practical future-state capture guidance.</span>
					</div>
				</div>
			</article>

			<article class="detail-section">
				<p class="card-kicker">Implementation</p>
				<h2>What the engagement covered</h2>
				<p>The project included workbook review, source-risk identification, cleaned dataset creation, row-by-row validation, exploratory analysis, formula checks, executive deck development, and lightweight future-state process recommendations.</p>
				<p>Validation mattered as much as analysis. The work explicitly restored excluded special-service records, corrected event-level discrepancies, and kept unresolved issues visible instead of overstating precision the source data could not support.</p>
			</article>

			<article class="detail-section">
				<p class="card-kicker">Engineering decisions</p>
				<h2>Why the approach stayed disciplined</h2>
				<ul class="detail-list">
					<li>Validate source trustworthiness before building interpretive reporting.</li>
					<li>Use cleaned analytical datasets as controlled working layers rather than relying on ad hoc workbook cleanup during presentation development.</li>
					<li>Recommend a lightweight future-state operating model because better capture design was a more immediate need than more sophisticated tooling.</li>
				</ul>
			</article>

			<article class="detail-section">
				<p class="card-kicker">Tradeoffs</p>
				<h2>What the solution did not pretend</h2>
				<p>A more complex data platform would have been unnecessary for the real problem. The constraint was not platform scale. It was source quality and process design. The work therefore favored clarity and defensibility over overengineering.</p>
				<p>Some source totals could not be fully reconciled, and the reporting had to remain honest about that. Protecting credibility was more important than forcing a cleaner story.</p>
			</article>

			<article class="detail-section">
				<p class="card-kicker">Outcome</p>
				<h2>What changed</h2>
				<p>The organization gained defensible reporting inputs, clearer leadership-facing analysis, and a realistic path toward more sustainable recurring analytics. Stakeholders could see value in trends and recurring metrics without overclaiming what the underlying data could prove.</p>
				<p>That outcome matters because many analytics efforts fail when teams rush past source-trust issues and treat presentation as a substitute for validation.</p>
			</article>

			<article class="detail-section">
				<p class="card-kicker">Lessons learned</p>
				<h2>Why this remains useful portfolio evidence</h2>
				<p>This project demonstrates validation-first analytics, stakeholder communication, and practical operating-model design. It shows how to create trustworthy reporting without overstating the maturity of the underlying data environment.</p>
				<p>It is secondary portfolio work because it is lighter-weight than the enterprise case studies, but it still reinforces core strengths around trust, remediation, and business-facing delivery.</p>
			</article>
